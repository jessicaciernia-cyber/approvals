C137 slides

Ready paper

Caption

Your intake is not a form. It is the product.

It is the first thing a patient meets and the last thing a reviewer reads. Most brands treat it as paperwork and hand it to whoever built the site.

It decides three things at once. Whether the patient finishes. Whether the provider can act without a second round of questions. Whether the record holds up when someone asks how the decision was made.

A form asks questions. An intake carries a decision, and it has to carry it all the way to the prescription without anyone retyping it.

Build it once and every category after the first one gets cheaper.

C147 slides

Four questions to ask a pharmacy partner before you sign

Ready coral

Founders price pharmacy as a line item. It behaves like a dependency, and you find that out on the first bad week rather than the first good one.

Caption

Four questions to ask a pharmacy partner before you sign.

Founders price pharmacy as a line item. It behaves like a dependency, and you find that out on the first bad week rather than the first good one.

One. What can actually be dispensed, in what forms.

Two. Which states are covered, and which ones lapse first.

Three. What turnaround looks like when the queue is full, not when it is empty.

Four. What happens when something is out of stock.

The last one is the real test. Every pharmacy looks the same until something is unavailable. Ask it before you sign, because after you sign it stops being a question and starts being your problem.

C157 slides

Caption

One patient, four handoffs, three systems.

The patient does not see three systems. They see one brand that keeps asking them the same questions.

Intake to provider. Provider to pharmacy. Pharmacy to fulfilment. Fulfilment back to refill.

Every handoff is a place to lose them, and a place to lose the record.

The last one decides retention. If the refill is manual, the relationship is manual, and manual relationships end quietly.

One record, or four versions of a person.

C167 slides

Why founders rebuild in month nine

Ready paper

Caption

Why founders rebuild in month nine.

Nothing broke. The thing they built simply ran out of room.

Month one is one category and one state and one flow. Month nine is three categories, a dozen states, a compliance question nobody owns and a refill nobody automated.

The rebuild is not a mistake. It is a deadline, and it arrives on schedule for anybody who launched narrow.

You cannot add a second protocol to a platform that was designed around the first one. You can only rebuild around both.

Build for the second protocol on day one, and month nine is a launch instead of a rescue.

C177 slides

Fifty states is not a marketing problem

Ready coral

Caption

Fifty states is not a marketing problem.

It is a licensing one, and it does not respond to budget.

You can run ads into a state you cannot serve. The traffic arrives either way. The prescription does not.

Coverage takes four things, and every one of them has its own clock. A licensed provider in that state. Credentialing on file. Malpractice that travels. A pharmacy that ships there.

None of that speeds up because the campaign is live.

Coverage gets built before it gets advertised. Otherwise the media buy is a tour of places you cannot fill.

Caption

Compliance is the moat, not the cost.

Founders budget for it the way they budget for insurance. Something you pay for and hope never matters.

It is the opposite. It is the reason a competitor with more money still cannot enter your category next quarter.

The pharmacy relationship, the provider network, the state coverage, the ad account that stays open. Every one of those is slow to earn and instant to lose, which is exactly what a moat is.

Nobody loses an account for one caption. They lose it for a pattern the caption finally made visible.

Built in, it is a barrier to entry. Bolted on, it is a bill.
