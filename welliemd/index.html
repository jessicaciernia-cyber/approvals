C16 slide 4 says "100% LegitScript pass rate." That is an approval rate and it implies WellieMD obtains a client's certification. The brief bars both.

5 signs you're ready to launch a telehealth brand, even if you don't feel ready.

Why one-drug brands die: the retention math

Check a claim black

Weight-loss-only against multi-protocol, argued on lifetime value rather than on clinical claims.

Slide 4 'Same acquisition cost. 10x the lifetime value.' A hard multiple with nothing attached. The brief bars new statistics.
Caption

Why one-drug brands die: the retention math.

A weight-loss-only patient stays a few months. Then the drug stops, or the brand does, and the revenue line follows.

A hormone-care patient stays for years. Protocols evolve. Care compounds. So does the relationship.

The acquisition cost is the same either way. What changes is how long you keep what you paid for.

Smart brands use one door and many rooms. Weight care in front, hormones and longevity behind it.

Retention isn't a marketing trick. It's an infrastructure choice, made before the first patient arrives.

Caption

From idea to live patients. Here's the actual sequence.

Brand and offer first. Name, treatments, pricing structure, locked with strategy rather than guesswork.

Then the build. Intake flows, patient portal, prescribing logic, configured rather than coded from scratch.

Then providers and pharmacy. Licensed clinicians assigned and fulfillment routed.

Then the compliance pass. LegitScript preparation, ad-account readiness, state rules, engineered in rather than bolted on.

Then launch. First patients, first prescriptions, live revenue.

Most founders spend a year getting this order wrong before they learn the shortcut, which is that the order is the shortcut. Don't build it alone.

Caption

Peptides are one category with five customer types, and each one behaves like its own product line.

Peptides are signaling molecules. Short chains of amino acids, being studied across nearly every system in the body.

Recovery is where most patients enter. Tissue repair is the most searched corner of the category.

Sleep is next, and it is the one nobody plans a product line around.

Skin arrives through beauty budgets and stays because it is being studied clinically.

Longevity is the one patients ask about first and the one most platforms cannot support.

Five entry points. One record, one intake, one brand behind all of them. That is the difference between selling a protocol and building a category.

5 myths keeping smart founders out of telehealth.

Myth one: I need to be a doctor. You need licensed providers, and they already exist in all fifty states.

Myth two: it takes years and millions. Not on existing infrastructure, and not with a custom tech build you were never going to need.

Myth three: compliance is impossible. It isn't impossible, it's engineered. The work is real and it is done before launch rather than after an audit.

Myth four: the market is too crowded. Weight loss is crowded. Hormones, peptides and longevity are wide open.

Myth five: platforms own your patients. Not here. The brand, the list and the upside stay yours.

Every myth survives because it used to be true. Infrastructure changed the rules.

The only real barrier left is starting.

How big an audience do you actually need to launch a health brand?

Check a claim magenta

Answers the objection that stops most practitioners: that they need reach before they can start. Argues trust over size.

How big an audience do you actually need to launch a health brand? Smaller than you think.

The myth is that you need a million followers. The math is that you need trust, not reach.

A thousand people who genuinely trust you, paired with a subscription that actually cares for them, is a real healthcare business. Not a big one on day one. A real one.

Conversion beats scale, every time. An engaged niche outconverts a cold million, because the cold million was never going to ask you a health question in the first place.

What matters more than size is health-adjacent trust. Patients, clients, community. People who already come to you with questions.

That audience is the launch asset. The infrastructure is the rest, and it already exists.
