C18Tue Sep 15 7 slides

The question nobody asks out loud: what happens at month six

Ready nightBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “I'll be six figures down at month six with nothing happening”

Caption

The question nobody asks out loud on a discovery call: what happens if I'm six months in and it isn't working?

It is the right question. Ask it early, and ask it of anyone you are considering.

Nobody can promise you an outcome. Anyone who does is telling you something they cannot know. What you can ask for is the mechanism: what gets looked at, how often, and what changing course actually involves.

Month one is not a revenue month. It is a data month. What did traffic cost, who filled out the form, where did they stop.

By month three you are not asking whether it worked. You are asking which part is not working, because the funnel tells you: the ad, the landing page, the intake, or the offer.

The founders who lose money are not the ones whose first attempt underperformed. They are the ones who kept spending without changing anything, because nobody was reading the numbers with them.

Ask what the review looks like before you ask what the return looks like.

Ready paperBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “The sticker price isn't the price”

Caption

The build fee is not the whole number, and you should find that out on the first call rather than the fourth.

There are four separate things you pay for in a telehealth launch, and they start at different times.

The build. One time. Brand, site, intake, provider and pharmacy connections, compliance work.

The platform. Recurring, and it usually begins after the build ends rather than on day one. This is what runs orders, records and messaging.

The CRM. Recurring. What holds the patient list and the follow-up.

Ad spend. Recurring, and this one is not a fee at all. It goes to Meta or Google, not to the agency. If your quote blurs ad spend and ad management into one line, ask which is which.

Then the cost of goods on every order, which comes out of revenue rather than out of pocket.

None of that is hidden. It is just rarely presented in one place before someone has already decided how they feel.

C20Thu Sep 17 7 slides

Nobody can give you a revenue projection. Here is what you can model instead.

Ready nightBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “Show me the numbers”

Caption

If you ask a telehealth vendor what you will make, a good one will decline. That is not evasion. Revenue projections in this category are regulated, and anyone happy to give you one is guessing with your money.

So here is what you can model yourself, before you sign anything.

What it costs to get one patient. Ask to see real ad accounts, not a case study slide.

What you keep on one order. Price, minus the medication, minus the provider fee, minus shipping.

How long a patient stays. This is the number most founders never ask for, and it is the one that decides whether the business works.

How many orders you need to cover fixed costs. Divide what you keep per order into what you pay per month.

Four numbers. Nobody can hand you the fifth one, which is what you will actually make, because it depends on your offer, your niche and what you do in month two.

If you cannot get those four out of a vendor, that is the answer.

C23Tue Sep 22 7 slides

Legal for the company is not the same as legal for you

Ready nightBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “Is this legal, and what am I liable for?”

Caption

The first question a good prospect asks is not what it costs. It is whether this is legal, and specifically whether it is legal for them.

Those are two different questions and only one of them usually gets answered.

The platform being compliant is table stakes. What you want to know is which obligations land on you, by name.

Four things have to exist before an order can be taken. A pharmacy that can dispense what you sell. Providers licensed in the states you sell into. Certification that lets you process payments and advertise at all. And an intake that produces a record defensible if anyone asks how a decision was made.

Buying a build means someone assembles those and keeps them current. It does not mean they transfer to you. The brand on the box is yours, and so is the obligation to keep the pieces in place.

Ask any vendor which of the four is theirs, which is yours, and what happens to each if you leave. If they cannot answer that cleanly, that is your answer.

C24Wed Sep 23 7 slides

Revenue does not start when the site goes live. It starts when the last gate clears.

Ready paperBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “When does money actually start coming in?”

Caption

Everyone asks how long until money comes in. It is the right instinct and the wrong question, because the answer is not a date. It is a list.

Revenue does not start when the website goes live. It starts when the last gate clears, and the gates do not run in parallel.

You cannot take payment for a medication that has not been prescribed. So the provider has to be in place before the order exists.

You cannot advertise, or in most cases process payments at all, without certification. So that has to clear before traffic is worth buying.

You cannot fill an order without a pharmacy that carries what you sell, in the state the patient lives in.

Which is why launching ads early is the most expensive mistake in this category. You are paying for attention you cannot convert, and the attention does not wait.

Ask what the gates are and which one is furthest out. That tells you more than any timeline will.

C28Tue Sep 29 7 slides

Owning the patients is not a sentence in a contract. It is four questions.

Ready coralBuilt from the sales calls

Answers the objection: “Do I own the patients, or do you?”

Caption

Every telehealth platform will tell you that you own your patients. It costs nothing to say. Here is how you find out whether it is true.

Where does the list live? If you cannot export it today, without asking, you do not have it. You have access to it.

Can you see the conversations? Support, provider messages, the moment someone hesitated. If the platform shows you outcomes but not exchanges, you own a spreadsheet, not a relationship.

What happens on the day you leave? Not whether you can leave. What comes with you, in what format, and how long it takes.

Who holds the medical records? This is the one honest answer nobody likes. Medical records follow the prescriber, not the brand. Any platform claiming otherwise is telling you something that is not theirs to give.

Ownership is not a clause. It is four answers, and you can get all four before you sign anything.
