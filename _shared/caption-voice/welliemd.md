# WellieMD caption voice

Source: the WellieMD revision brief as applied on the approval pages (welliemd-carousels-new/write_hub.py footer), and the nine captions and flag notes already on the site (_data.json). Nothing here is invented.

## Rules, verbatim

The brief bars: no launch timeframes, no new statistics or third-party data, no approval rates, nothing implying WellieMD obtains a client's certification, no thyroid.

## How the captions read

Founder-to-founder, plain, declarative. Short paragraphs, one idea each. No emoji. No hashtags on the existing captions; a draft ends with three to five proposed tags the owner can delete. No questions stacked at the end. A caption states the mechanism, then the consequence, then the point.

## Real captions

### 
```
Every telehealth brand you admire runs on the same four systems.

Intake. Providers. Pharmacy. Compliance.

Nobody chooses a brand for its intake form, which is exactly why founders underestimate what sits behind one.

Miss any one of the four and the other three stop mattering. A provider network with no pharmacy behind it cannot fill. A pharmacy with no compliance posture cannot advertise.

The stack is not the thing you sell. It is the thing that lets you sell anything at all.
```

### 
```
5 signs you're ready to launch a telehealth brand, even if you don't feel ready.

One. You have an audience that trusts you. Patients, followers, clients, a list. Trust is the hardest asset to build and you already have it.

Two. You keep referring people to products you don't own. Every referral is revenue you built and handed away.

Three. You know your niche's problem better than the brands serving it. Insight is the unfair advantage no platform can buy.

Four. You've built a business before, any business. Operating skill transfers. Healthcare is a category, not a different species.

Five. You're done waiting for permission from an industry that never gives it.

Nobody hands out healthcare brands. Founders take the step.
```

### 
```
Why one-drug brands die: the retention math.

A weight-loss-only patient stays a few months. Then the drug stops, or the brand does, and the revenue line follows.

A hormone-care patient stays for years. Protocols evolve. Care compounds. So does the relationship.

The acquisition cost is the same either way. What changes is how long you keep what you paid for.

Smart brands use one door and many rooms. Weight care in front, hormones and longevity behind it.

Retention isn't a marketing trick. It's an infrastructure choice, made before the first patient arrives.
```

## Flag vocabulary

Two levels only. `check` = Check a claim (needs a source or a confirmation before it ships). `stop` = One slide cannot ship (breaks a brief rule outright). Every flag names the slide number and quotes the words on the slide.
